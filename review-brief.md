# Nepal MHPSS IEC — consultation brief

Draft samples for communications and technical consultation. Not approved for public health distribution; no WHO endorsement is claimed. This public review page is accessible without login; no confidential feedback should be posted here.

## Purpose and scope

Audience: adults and caregivers affected by floods in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and surrounding communities. Proposed communication outcome: recognize that reactions can vary, choose a feasible support action, and know when additional or urgent help is needed. This is a proposed outcome, not a tested effect.

Included: one English A3 common-reactions poster, eight fictional community character candidates and four professional-character candidates (female/male nurse and psychosocial-counsellor pairs). Excluded: working archives, source code/ZIPs, previous poster iterations, clinical/client data, and original Myanmar/Syria/EDCD reference files. Fictional cast is not a claim to represent every Nepal community; role/gender examples are non-exhaustive.

## Production transparency

Illustrations were generated with AI (cx/gpt-image-2.5) under human-directed briefs and digitally composed with separately typeset text. They are not documentary photographs or testimony from affected people. Dialogue is fictional. Characters, names, uniforms, expressions and cultural suitability require local review. AI-assisted production is disclosed for review; applicable WHO/Nepal-office policy on its use has not been established. No automated tool can certify clinical accuracy, cultural acceptability or non-triggering for everyone.

## Verified public WHO guidance

WHO’s communication framework sets out six principles: accessible, actionable, credible/trusted, relevant, timely and understandable.[1]

WHO’s plain-language guidance recommends putting important information first, defining a single overarching communication outcome, breaking complex information into manageable portions and preserving white space.[2]

WHO’s visual guidance recommends illustrations that clarify sequences, relationships and steps in a process.[3]

WHO recommends messages in languages understood by target audiences, country/community adaptation and support for local-language communication; it also encourages original non-English messages, not only translation from English.[4] The current English sample is for consultation, not a substitute for locally developed/reviewed Nepali or other relevant languages.

WHO states that its logo cannot be used without written authorization; licences/permissions must be checked for each reused WHO asset.[5] This consultation package uses no WHO logo and links to official guidance rather than reproducing protected identity assets.

The public WHO framework, printed p. 18, states that technical and communications teams cross-check/clear products and refers to WHO eManual section VIII for planning, production and logo clearance.[6] Internal eManual, current office-specific workflow and applicable internal style/AI rules were not accessed. Public guidance does not establish that these samples are compliant or cleared.

## Questions for WHO Comms and technical reviewers

1. Is the communication outcome sufficiently focused, and is A3 the right channel/format versus a short series or facilitated flipchart?
2. Are fictional recurring characters and this disclosed AI-assisted illustration workflow permissible? What rights documentation, disclosure and human-illustrator review are required?
3. Which country-office/MoHP/partner clearance, authorship, branding, acknowledgement and production process applies? Please identify the current internal templates/style guidance.
4. Is the wording on common reactions, voluntary sharing, grounding, medication continuity and urgent help appropriate? Should the self-harm/footer content be redesigned as a separate companion asset?
5. Which Nepali and additional community languages, cultural reviewers and intended-reader groups should inform adaptation and comprehension/acceptability testing?
6. Are character names, appearance, diversity, age reading, uniforms and role boundaries appropriate? PFA, psychosocial counselling, nursing, psychology and psychiatry must not be interchangeable.
7. Which routine/urgent referral pathways can be verified at release, including safe access, language/disability access and backup when roads or phones fail?
8. What print/phone checks, field testing, revision control, approval and institutional handover steps are required before distribution?
